# 🏦 v0.5.0 — Stable-amount model

[← Index](README.md) · [GitHub milestone](https://github.com/danielep71/VBA-IRRBB-Toolkit/milestone/5) · [Previous](v0.4.0.md) · [Next →](v0.6.0.md)

## 🎯 Purpose

Estimate the persistence of existing balances and the corresponding stable
amount over multiple periods. The model links account transitions to
observable drivers and aggregates results using an explicit definition
of population, weights, and horizon.

## 💡 Why it matters

An aggregate balance can remain constant because new deposits offset
outflows from existing accounts. This does not establish that the opening
balance remained available. Stable must distinguish survival of existing
amounts from growth and new inflows, preventing missing-data or weighting
rules from creating artificial stability.

## 🛠️ Activities and deliverables

1. **Build transitions.** Apply approved rules for balances, cash-out,
   verified closures, observation gaps, the end of the sample, and migrations.
   Make the transitions admitted to estimation observable.
2. **Prepare predictors.** Use only information available at the forecast
   origin under #32. Record transformations and contract-defined handling
   of missing values.
3. **Estimate the model.** Implement balance-weighted fractional logit,
   predictor selection, and robust account-clustered covariance using
   reproducible conventions defined before development.
4. **Handle failures.** Separation, nonconvergence, and singular systems must
   produce diagnostics without saving apparently valid parameters for
   subsequent use.
5. **Aggregate survival.** Calculate shares and amounts by account or cohort
   and by segment/currency, with explicit weight evolution. Preserve the
   relationship between opening amount, remaining share, and runoff.

## 🔎 Practical example: why weights matter

Two cohorts have opening balances of 50 each and constant survival shares
of 0.9 and 0.1. After two periods, summing the individual cohorts gives
`50 × 0.9² + 50 × 0.1² = 41`. Applying the initial average of 0.5 twice
to the total instead gives `100 × 0.5² = 25`.

This synthetic case belongs to prerequisite #34. It shows that the two
operations represent different quantities. The contract must select and
justify the appropriate aggregation; this guide does not approve an
alternative formula in place of that amendment.

## 🔗 Dependencies and preliminary decisions

The [v0.2.0](v0.2.0.md) data workflow and tests, #32, and verified common
numerical primitives are required. Before the affected implementation,
#34 must specify the estimand, weights, aggregation, covariance,
finite-sample adjustments, selection, and boundary-case behavior.

Rates may supply rate paths in the later integrated workflow, but standalone
Stable tests must also work with known synthetic paths. Composition with
Decay separately requires #37.

## ✅ Completion criteria

- Coefficients and covariance are compared with an independent external
  reference, with documented versions, settings, and tolerances.
- Hand-constructed cases verify transitions, survival, weight evolution,
  and aggregation across multiple periods.
- Tests prevent future information from entering predictors and distinguish
  closure, missing data, and end-of-sample censoring.
- Estimation failures do not expose new valid parameters or make results
  from a previous run appear current.
- Compilation, regressions, and failure paths run in Excel on the exact
  commit with reproducible diagnostics.

## 🚧 Boundaries and tracking

Survival of existing balances is not a forecast of total deposit growth.
Statistical shares and regulatory constraints remain separate.
Issue [#34](https://github.com/danielep71/VBA-IRRBB-Toolkit/issues/34)
covers amendments and references. Implementation completion requires its
own activities and evidence; it cannot be inferred from closing the contract
issue alone.
